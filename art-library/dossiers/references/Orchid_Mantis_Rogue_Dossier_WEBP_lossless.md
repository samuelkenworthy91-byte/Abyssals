2. Family overview
This family is based on an orchid mantis, beginning as a small cute Bug-type and evolving into a Bug/Fire line with a strong fantasy rogue identity. The final fantasy should feel like a high-fantasy arcane trickster or dagger rogue: quick, elegant, theatrical and deadly. Its foreclaws are treated like flaming daggers, and repeated precise strikes should be central to both its visuals and its battle gameplay.

4. Locked visual guidance
Stage 1 — Orchidit
Keep the cute orchid-mantis look: pink and white petals, large magenta eyes, small body, soft elegance.
The design should read as delicate, observant and slightly mischievous rather than aggressive.
No fire typing yet; this stage should look floral and bug-like rather than martial.
Its battle art should remain very close to the included PNG.
Stage 2 — Cinderchid
This is the transition stage where the fantasy rogue idea begins to appear.
More upright and more anthropomorphic than stage 1, but still clearly part of the same family.
The forearms should feel like dagger-arms, with flame licking around the edges of the scythe-like claws.
This stage should look agile and self-assured, like a nimble skirmisher learning to fight with style.
Stage 3 — Roguorchid
This is the final approved battle-sprite design. Keep the pink-white orchid palette and flaming curved blades.
The final should feel like a high-fantasy rogue or arcane trickster: elegant posture, precise killer energy, theatrical movement, and intelligent malice rather than brute force.
Its claws should be treated as paired flaming daggers or short curved blades, with petals and embers trailing from slashes.
Even though a later rogue redraw exists, keep this original stage-3 design as the visual lock for this dossier.
5. Battle role and stat direction
The line should emphasise Speed, Attack and repeated-strike momentum. Bulk should stay light to moderate, especially on the final stage. The final form should not be tanky; it should win by speed, pressure and crit conversion.

6. Signature ability concept
Ability name: Assassin’s Cadence
Effect: Every consecutive successful use of the same damaging move by this Abyssal raises that move’s critical-hit chance by +10 percentage points for this user. This bonus stacks up to +30 percentage points total. The chain resets if the user selects a different move, misses, fails to act, or leaves the field.
Design purpose: reward repeated precise strikes and sell the rogue / combo-killer identity.
If Work prefers implementing it as critical-ratio stages rather than raw percentage, preserve the same feel: each consecutive use should materially increase crit reliability, with a clear ceiling.
Recommended users: ideally stage 2 and stage 3, or at minimum stage 3. Stage 1 can have a simpler standard ability if needed.
7. Signature move
Move name: Blossom Shiv
Type: Fire | Category: Physical | Power: 75 | Accuracy: 100 | PP: 15
Effect: High critical-hit ratio. If this move lands a critical hit, the user’s Speed rises by 1 stage after damage.
Design note: Blossom Shiv is the ideal “repeat this move to build toward crits” button, pairing directly with Assassin’s Cadence.
8. Move animation guidance for Work
Style goal: higher-detail Abyssals move VFX, readable pixel-art framing, elegant but dangerous.
Animation should feel like a rogue blink-dash or deceptive flourish, not a brute-force lunge.
Suggested frame flow: poised stance → quick vanish/feint → twin flaming claw-daggers arc across the target → petal scatter → ember ribbon trails → finishing cut with a bright crit flash if relevant.
Palette: warm pinks, white-hot orange, rose fire, pale gold and soft ember-red.
Visual motifs: orchid petals, dagger arcs, slashing crescents, controlled flame ribbons, and a clean finishing flare.
For critical hits, add an extra sharp flash and a final petal-burst / flame bloom accent.
9. Learnset direction
Work can choose exact move IDs, but the family identity should be preserved across the line.
Suggested move flavour by stage
Stage 1: light Bug slashes, feints, speed utility and floral-themed nuisance moves.
Stage 2: first Fire attacks, dagger-like physical attacks, evasive tricks and setup tools.
Stage 3: stronger Bug and Fire slash moves, critical-hit tools, Speed setup, and one or two rogue utility techniques such as evasive or repositioning support.
Recommended mechanical themes
Priority on physical moves over special moves.
One or two moves with naturally elevated crit chance.
At least one self-buffing speed option.
A learnset that encourages repeating a signature attack instead of constantly rotating through coverage.
10. Habitat and field journal guidance
Suggested early habitat for Orchidit: lush floral routes, orchid groves, hidden gardens or humid blossom clearings.
Suggested later habitat for Cinderchid / Roguorchid: warmer flower regions, ember orchards, volcanic gardens or twilight blossom routes.
Field Journal tone: elegant predator disguised as a flower-spirit; beautiful, deceptive and startlingly lethal when it commits to a strike.
11. Shiny direction
Recommended shiny palette: white petals with deeper crimson or wine-red markings, cooler blue or violet flames, and darker green accents.
Alternative option: black-rose and pale ivory petals with ghostly lilac fire.
Whatever Work chooses, the shiny should preserve the floral beauty but shift the fire into a more magical or sinister register.
