# Faces of the Expedition — 0.90.16

Dialogue previously invented `assets/<species ID>.webp` for atlas-backed species and `assets/<encounter ID>.webp` for aliased trainers. Companion dialogue now uses `combatArtSrc` and waits for the same decoding/framing/shiny path used by battles. A stale promise cannot overwrite the next speaker. Trainer dialogue resolves the actual portrait ID before selecting its loaded source. Failed images are hidden instead of showing the browser's broken-image glyph. Existing deliberate farm-bust SVG framing is retained.

Thirteen complete figures were recovered from the repo's archived character references, after reading `CHARACTER-CATALOG.json`, the confirmed ID index and archive README. Legacy names/codes are provenance, not assignments of current canon. These figures now distinguish Marasen's six road challengers and two fort guards, Botrune's three cordon captains and the mountain sentry/guardian. Only optimized selected WebPs enter the runtime; the original archive remains untouched. `character-art-provenance.json` records each exact source and source SHA-256. Magenta keys and detached sheet labels are excluded; the figures themselves are retained.

Khalid has a full-body battle continuation of his established cream-turban, teal/brass-armoured, green-cloaked design; his dialogue portrait and walking atlas remain the established ones. Canon Fatima remains the same female ruler, with the user's requested heavy build, broad shoulders, harsher face and imposing execution-leader stance. Existing story, pronouns and permanent decisions are preserved. Both were produced with built-in ImageGen from their existing runtime portraits; sources are archived outside `dist/` and the selected optimized WebPs are in `dist/assets/`. No new story characters or combat behavior are introduced.

Tests audit all creature source paths, restored trainer mapping across dialogue/battle/overworld, Khalid's separate battle path, aliased post-fight/rematch portraits, real image decoding, atlas and shiny companion conversations, and full-body trainer introductions. The first ten shiny previews are derived using the actual `oppositeColours` function, not image generation.
